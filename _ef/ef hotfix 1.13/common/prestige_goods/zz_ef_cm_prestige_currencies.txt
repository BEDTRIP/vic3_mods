### E&F Compat -- hand-maintained (was generated; generator retired 02.09.2026, see EF.6).
### base_good retargeted from spe_uni_c to liquidity_currency after author's own
### currency merge (E&F update 02.09.2026), see EF.1/EF.4.
###
### Three prestige variants of the shared currency good, one per monetary
### regime. Every central bank company carries all three; the good's own
### `possible` decides which one a country actually mints.
###
### WHY THREE AND NOT NINETY-FIVE. Only the first three declarations of a base
### good's variants become real slots; anything past them falls back to the
### third. Measured three times over: 3 declared -> all three correct; 10
### declared -> Britain kept the pound and every other country minted the franc,
### the third declaration; 95 declared -> the whole world minted the Iraqi
### dinar, again the third. Vanilla never exceeds three either, and no define
### controls it -- the number is in the executable.
###
### THE `possible` GATE IS UNPROVEN AT THIS SIZE. It was tried once with 95
### goods and appeared to do nothing, but that test was ruined by the count,
### and the gate was dropped before it was ever tried at three. Vanilla writes
### nothing but has_dlc_feature here, so the files cannot say whether it is
### evaluated in country scope. If it is, changing the monetary system law
### changes the minted currency by itself -- no company swap, no rebuild, no
### ownership lost. If it is not, every country will mint the metallic one and
### the answer is that regimes have to be split across companies instead.
###
### law_no_monetary_system has no entry on purpose: no monetary system means no
### central bank, so there is nothing to mint.

zz_ef_cm_representative_currency = {
	possible = {
		OR = {
			has_law = law_type:law_gold_standard
			has_law = law_type:law_silver_standard

		}
	}
